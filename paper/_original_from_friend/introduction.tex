\section{Introduction}

Video footage from match broadcasts and team cameras can be segmented into short clips of individual batting strokes, and automatically reading these clips to judge a player's technique and value is a practical and increasingly studied problem, used in coaching, scouting and broadcasting. The same stroke played by two different batters produces very different visual and statistical signatures, and turning that raw footage into a single, trustworthy, numbered analysis is where most existing systems fall short.

Most widely studied sports-analytics systems so far are built around a single narrow task for a single audience, such as predicting where the players on a team will move next \cite{ref1}, \cite{ref2}, detecting events directly from broadcast video \cite{ref3}, or training cooperative agents inside a simulated match \cite{ref4}, \cite{ref5}. A newer line of agentic systems, built from several language-model agents coordinating with one another, has shown real promise outside pure motion prediction, for example in supply chain decision-making \cite{ref6}, \cite{ref7}, and inside cricket itself to help fans assemble a fantasy team \cite{ref8}. However, each of these systems is still built around one task and one fixed answer format, released no mechanism for checking its own numbers against a trusted source before reporting them, and offers no way to serve more than one kind of cricket stakeholder from the same underlying system. This matters because a report that sounds confident but was never checked against real evidence can quietly mislead the very people, coaches, scouts, medical staff, who rely on it to make a decision. To address this critical gap, we introduce CricketMind, a single agentic system that turns a batting-shot video, or a plain cricket question with no video at all, into a checked, stakeholder-specific analytics report.

The main contributions of this research are outlined as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Development of CricketMind, an agentic pipeline that classifies a batting shot from 15 sampled video frames using a vision encoder and a temporal transformer, or bypasses the video path entirely for text-only questions, with an automatic confidence-based retry loop so that a low-confidence shot label is never silently passed downstream.
    \item A shared, structured Shot DNA record and two sanctioned calculation engines, a deterministic statistics engine and a Monte Carlo what-if simulator, that hold exclusive authority to produce a number, drawing evidence from two separate retrieval indexes, a precise ball-by-ball index and a broader historical index, so that no other component in the system is able to fabricate a statistic.
    \item A dynamic supervisor router that wakes only the relevant subset of specialized stakeholder-facing agents, covering audiences from coaches and players to medical staff, scouts and broadcasters, out of twelve agents in total, keeping every generated report focused on the question actually asked.
    \item A faithfulness gate that cross-checks every numeric claim made by an activated agent against the values the two calculation engines actually produced, and forces that agent to regenerate its answer whenever a mismatch is found, before a supervisor LLM composes the final, stakeholder-specific analytics report.
\end{itemize}
