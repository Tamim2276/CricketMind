\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduced CricketMind, an agentic architecture that answers cricket
questions, with or without a batting-shot clip, through twelve stakeholder
agents whose every cited figure must come from one of two sanctioned engines
and is verified before release. Implemented as a LangGraph state graph, its
cost followed a closed-form model that predicted all seven measured scenarios
exactly, with worst-case bounds of 29 supersteps and 12 agent invocations fixed
when the graph is constructed. On twelve paraphrased queries that avoid every
routing keyword, a locally hosted 7B language-model router activated the
intended agent nine times where a keyword router activated it none, with
identical decisions across three runs, and the fixed rules that follow its
proposal preserve every bound. The Faith Check rejected fabricated citations
but not a correct figure attached to the wrong subject or a figure left
uncited, making a subject-keyed citation contract a requirement for
language-model agents rather than an implementation detail. CricketMind thus
offers a verifiable foundation on which the stand-in components can be
replaced by learned ones without weakening its guarantees.
