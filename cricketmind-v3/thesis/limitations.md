# Limitations and threats to validity

Write a weakness down the day you notice it. A reviewer will find it anyway, and the paper is
stronger when it names it first.

| ID | Limitation | Why it matters | What the paper should say or do | Noticed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L-001 | All design-time examples use the v2 synthetic corpus | Says nothing about real players | Label as illustrative; real-data results come from Phase 10 | 2026-10-16 |
| L-002 | The probability bands come from Kent (1964), written for intelligence analysts | Cricket audiences may read words differently | Cite; call the bands a design choice; future work: a reader study | 2026-10-16 |
| L-003 | The workload ratio's validity is disputed (Impellizzeri et al. 2021), and match data omits training loads | Medical claims could mislead | The workload output is a monitoring flag, never an injury probability | 2026-10-16 |
| L-004 | A licensed commentary corpus with match and ball keys may not exist | The shot bridge may be impossible | Go/no-go gate; fallback evaluation set; report a negative result honestly | 2026-10-16 |
| L-005 | Weak shot labels carry noise, and joining them to deliveries is error-prone (wides, no-balls) | Shot statistics inherit the errors | Report κ, per-class F1 and join rate; carry label confidence | 2026-10-16 |
| L-006 | Pose estimation may fail on low-resolution clips; posture has no ground truth | Posture claims could be wrong | Validate against a proxy (front vs back foot); drop metrics below AUC 0.75 | 2026-10-16 |
| L-007 | Bowling type is missing for some players | Filters such as "vs pace" silently shrink n | Always report n; evidence floor C3; report coverage | 2026-10-16 |
| L-008 | A 7B local model may break the JSON schema | Findings fail to parse | Constrained decoding; count parse failures as C1 rejections and report them | 2026-10-16 |
| L-009 | The routing questions and their labels are written by the author | Optimistic bias | Include paraphrases; ideally a second labeller; say it plainly | 2026-10-16 |
| L-010 | Thresholds (n_min = 30 balls, 10 innings, 20 reviews; κ, AUC, caps) are design parameters, not tuned values | Results depend on them | Name them as such; a sensitivity check if time allows | 2026-10-16 |
| L-011 | Measurements are made on one machine | Timings may not transfer | Report hardware; prefer counts (supersteps, calls) over seconds | 2026-10-16 |
| L-012 | The human study is small (about 10 raters) and not a field deployment | Usefulness to real stakeholders is only lightly tested | Report it as a small study with its numbers; claims stay mainly about verification; a field study is future work | 2026-10-16 (narrowed 2026-10-19 by D-019) |
| L-013 | Tone is checked only through C7's word list; words not on the list, sarcasm and subtle framing are not | A correct, tagged number can still be framed misleadingly | Say plainly that v3 guarantees numbers, subjects, certainty words and listed tone words, not every nuance; numbers always carry their own label; count the misses in the Phase 10 runs | 2026-10-17 (narrowed by D-017) |
| L-014 | Rules explanations (the Explainer) are not checked against any source | An explanation can be wrong | Label every explanation "not checked against data"; no digits or player names in it; a checked rules source (the MCC Laws) is future work | 2026-10-17 |
| L-015 | N3 can misread a question, especially an unusual wording | A correct answer to the wrong question | Every answer starts "I read this as: …"; measured on the coverage set, with 5 wordings per question | 2026-10-17 |
| L-016 | CricBench's questions may not be released in time, and its databases may come from a different Cricsheet snapshot | The outside test may be impossible or slightly unfair | Ask the authors early (Day 14); match snapshots or report the difference; if never released, say so | 2026-10-19 |
| L-017 | The football case study is small: one open data source, about 10 measures, three agents, no video | Generality is shown, not proven for every sport | Call it a case study; report exactly what was reused and what was new | 2026-10-19 |
| L-018 | Online language models change over time | The online-model results may not repeat later | Record the model name, version and date; keep the local models as the main, repeatable results | 2026-10-19 |
| L-019 | The text checks read words from lists: C3's warnings, C4's band words and hedges, C6's names, C7's tone words | A word not on a list slips past C6 or C7 (a miss); a warning or hedge not on a list is rejected (a false alarm, the safe side) | Measure misses and false alarms on real findings (Days 151, 155); grow the lists from the practice set only, never from a locked exam | 2026-10-21 |
| L-020 | C5 catches digits only: a number spelled out in words ("one hundred and fifty") passes | An agent could type a number in words | "Six", "four", "fifty" and "hundred" are also cricket words, so a word list would reject honest sentences; report the gap, and count spelled-out numbers by hand in the human study | 2026-10-21 |
| L-021 | Sentences are split at '.', '!' and '?'; an abbreviation such as "e.g." ends a sentence early | A warning, hedge or comparison may be read as belonging to the wrong sentence | Agents are told to avoid abbreviations (Day 65 prompts); report any case found in the evaluation | 2026-10-21 |
| L-022 | For a difference, C7 reads a tone word as being about its first side: "struggles with the pull" beside cover drive minus pull is rejected | An honest judgment about the second side is a false alarm | Agents write the comparison with the judged side first (Day 65 prompts); count these false alarms (Day 155) | 2026-10-21 |
| L-023 | v2 mode re-implements v2's rule (exact equality on cited values), not v2's code; v2 matched each value by a fact name, our claims by value | E-004's v2 column is a reconstruction | Say so; the v2 code's own boundary experiment (E-001) agrees on F1 and F2 | 2026-10-21 |
| L-024 | Cricsheet withholds 380 matches: those of the Afghanistan men's team and of the Afghanistan Premier League | Afghan players' numbers cover only their other matches (e.g. franchise leagues elsewhere), and no claim about the Afghanistan men's team can be made | State it in the Data section; N3 gives a can't-check answer for questions about withheld matches | 2026-10-21 |
| L-025 | Phases follow a fixed rule per format, not the match: a rain-shortened match has a shorter powerplay than the rule says, The Hundred gets no phase, and a ball with two wickets keeps only the first one's details | Some balls in shortened matches carry the wrong phase | Counted on the full data (N-015): 2,901 of 38,277 limited-overs innings (7.6%) have a shortened powerplay, and 17 balls have two wickets; report both, and use the file's own powerplay when an engine needs the powerplay exactly | 2026-10-21 |
| L-026 | Batting hands and bowler types come from the cricketdata package, built from ESPNcricinfo's player pages; not every player is covered, some styles are missing or mistyped at the source, and "pace or spin" simplifies (a slow-medium seamer is pace) | Filters by bowler type or hand miss some balls, and a wrong source label would put a ball in the wrong group | Report coverage per format (Day 37 build); hand-label the bowlers with the most unlabelled balls; cite the package; check the source's terms before publishing derived data | 2026-10-21 |
| L-027 | A Test or first-class match is dated by its first day: a ball bowled on day four counts as day one | Workloads (M3) for multi-day matches are approximate by up to four days | Say so; workload flags are given for limited-overs cricket first | 2026-10-21 |
| L-028 | Ground names are merged by a rule (the name before the comma, kept apart when it comes with several places) | Two grounds with one name and no place could still be merged; one ground under two different names stays split (Day 40: M Chinnaswamy Stadium is three grounds in the data, "…, Bangalore", "…, Bengaluru" and "M.Chinnaswamy Stadium", so M2 gives three partial profiles) | Show the merged names (`names_used`); spot-check the grounds N3 is asked about; merge a ground's known other names (renamed cities, spellings) before N3 looks grounds up (Day 58) | 2026-10-21 |
| L-029 | A one-word name ("Babar") can mean several players; the most-played comes first | The answer may be about the wrong player | The read-back ("I read this as: …") shows the choice; N3 asks when two candidates are close | 2026-10-21 |
| L-030 | Seven matches disagree with themselves at the source: the file's target or winning margin differs from its balls by 1–3 runs (all associate matches, e.g. Nigeria v Tanzania, Kwibuka 2022) | Which is wrong, a ball or the summary, cannot be told from the file; a number about those teams could be a run out | Keep them as published, list them in the data report (N-015), and report them to Cricsheet | 2026-10-21 |
| L-031 | 161 player names belong to more than one player in the data (five "Imran Khan"s) | A question that names one of them cannot be answered from the name alone | The engine refuses such a name instead of guessing (D-040); N3 asks which one, and the subject then carries the register id (Day 58) | 2026-10-22 |
| L-032 | Cricsheet does not hold every match of a career: 118 of Kohli's 125 T20Is are in the snapshot (3,963 of his 4,188 runs) | A "career" number can fall short of the official record | Every number is about the matches in the data, with n shown; the engine report sets several players against published records where their matches are all there (E-026, Day 54) | 2026-10-22 |
| L-033 | Like with like is by format only: within a format, a player's matches can be stronger or weaker than everyone else's (the IPL against other T20s), and era, venue and opposition are not adjusted for | "Above everyone else" can partly reflect where a player played | Say "against everyone else's balls in the same formats, under the same filters"; finer adjustment is future work | 2026-10-22 |
| L-034 | A range from 20,000 replays is itself a little random: an end can be one step off the exact range (one run in 50 balls; the v2 example gives [−16, +78] against the exact [−16, +76]); and percentile ranges run narrow on small samples | A range end within one step of 0 could flip the verdict under another seed | The seed is fixed, so every result is reproducible; C3's floor keeps samples at 30 balls or more; the engine report counts how many real ranges end within one step of 0 (Day 54) | 2026-10-22 |
| L-035 | A ground's profile covers every season in the data: T20 scoring has risen over the years, so a first-innings average since 2006 sits below today's par | A captain reading "150.4 at Mirpur" may take it as today's par | Show the seasons covered; Day 43's season filter applies to M2 as well | 2026-10-22 |
| L-036 | The workload ratio compares a bowler only with himself: after weeks of heavy bowling, a heavy week looks usual. On 4 January 2025, the day Bumrah broke down at Sydney, his ratio was 1.32, "close to his usual week", after 626 balls in four weeks (N-018) | A physio could read "close to his usual week" as "safe" | The four weeks' balls always show beside the ratio; the words never say "normal" or "safe"; an absolute-load flag waits for a cut-off with evidence behind it | 2026-10-22 |
| L-037 | Each ball's day in a multi-day match is estimated: the match's balls are spread evenly over its dates. And matches missing from Cricsheet (domestic games, some internationals) are missing load | A ball can land a day early or late (rain, short days), and a bowler's load can be under-counted | Say "estimated" for multi-day matches; the week and four weeks are "in the matches in our data" | 2026-10-22 |
